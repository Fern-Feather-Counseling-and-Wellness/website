## Why This Course?

**The Problem:**
- 1 in 5 children have a learning or attention difference (CDC, 2023)
- Schools often lack the resources or training to support neurodivergent learners
- Parents are left to navigate a complex legal system without guidance
- Without advocacy, children miss critical interventions that shape their academic trajectory

**The Solution:**
This course empowers you with the knowledge, documents, and confidence to advocate effectively for your child—without needing a lawyer.

## Course Modules

### Module 1: Understanding Your Rights

**Learning Objectives:**
- Know the federal laws protecting your child's education
- Understand your state's specific regulations
- Document your concerns effectively

**1.1 The Laws That Protect Your Child**
- **IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act):** Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE), Individualized Education Programs (IEP), least restrictive environment (LRE)
- **Section 504:** Accommodations vs. special education, 504 plans
- **ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act):** Broader accessibility requirements
- **FERPA:** Your child's privacy rights

*Key Quote:* "IDEA is about FAPE—Free Appropriate Public Education. It's not about the best education. It's about an education that's reasonably calculated to enable your child to make progress appropriate in light of their circumstances." (Wrightslaw, 2023)

**1.2 Eligibility Categories**
- Specific Learning Disability (SLD)
- Other Health Impairment (OHI) — often used for ADHD
- Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)
- Emotional Disturbance
- Developmental Delay (ages 3-9)

**1.3 Understanding Your State's Standards**
- Find your state's DOE website
- Compare state regulations to federal minimums

**Templates:**

- *Glossary of Special Education Terms*

### Module 2: Documentation & Data Collection

**Learning Objectives:**
- Create a comprehensive record of your child's needs
- Use objective data to support your requests
- Organize documents for easy access

**2.1 Why Documentation Matters**
- Creates a paper trail
- Provides objective evidence
- Protects your child if disputes arise

**2.2 What to Document**
- Academic performance (grades, standardized tests)
- Behavioral observations (home vs. school)
- Medical and therapeutic records
- Communication logs (emails, letters)
- Teacher feedback and progress reports

**2.3 Creating a Tracking System**
- Google Drive / Dropbox organization
- Date-stamped entries
- Consistent format

**2.4 The "Baseline" Document**
- Creating a snapshot of current functioning
- Updated annually

### Module 3: Requesting Evaluations

**Learning Objectives:**
- Write effective evaluation requests
- Understand the referral process
- Know what to do if the school says "no"

**3.1 The Referral Process**
- Who can make a referral: Parents, teachers, school personnel
- The written request: What to include
- Timeline: 30-60 days (varies by state)

**3.2 Writing Your Request Letter**
- State your concerns clearly
- Include specific examples
- Request specific evaluations
- Use legal language where appropriate

**Sample Letter:**
> Dear [Principal/School Psychologist],
>
> I am writing to request a comprehensive educational evaluation for my child, [Child's Name], due to concerns regarding [attention/focus/learning/behavior/etc.]. I am requesting evaluations in the areas of [academic achievement/cognitive functioning/social-emotional/behavioral/occupational/speech-language, etc.].
>
> I am requesting this evaluation pursuant to the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and [Your State's] regulations. Please contact me to schedule a meeting to discuss the referral within 10 business days.
>
> Sincerely, [Your Name]

**3.3 If the School Refuses**
- Written denial = appeal rights
- Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE)
- Advocacy organizations for support

**3.4 Types of Evaluations**
- Psychoeducational (cognitive + achievement)
- Neuropsychological (more comprehensive)
- Functional Behavior Assessment (FBA)
- Speech-Language, OT, behavioral

**Templates:**
- *Evaluation Request Letter (Template)*
- *Follow-Up Letter (If No Response)*

### Module 4: The IEP Meeting

**Learning Objectives:**
- Prepare effectively for IEP meetings
- Understand your role as an equal team member
- Make informed decisions in the moment

**4.1 Preparing for the Meeting**
- Request all documents in advance (10 days before)
- Review current data and progress
- Prepare your list of concerns and requests
- Bring a support person (friend, advocate, attorney)

**4.2 IEP Team Roles**
- Parents (you!)
- General education teacher
- Special education teacher
- School psychologist
- Administrator
- Related service providers (OT, speech, counseling)

**4.3 What the IEP Must Include**
- Present levels of performance (PLOP)
- Annual goals (measurable)
- Services and minutes
- Placement/LRE
- Accommodations and modifications
- State assessments (including exemptions)

**4.4 Your Rights in the Meeting**
- Record the meeting (inform the school)
- Take breaks
- Request decisions be tabled
- Decline to sign until you understand
- Iterate: "We'll think about it"

**4.5 After the Meeting**
- Review the draft IEP thoroughly
- Request changes in writing
- Consent vs. non-consent
- Progress monitoring

**Templates:**
- *IEP Meeting Preparation Checklist*
- *IEP Goal Tracker*
- *Parent Input Statement Template*

### Module 5: Accommodations vs. Modifications

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand the difference between accommodations and modifications
- Request appropriate supports
- Monitor effectiveness

**5.1 Accommodations**
- *Definition:* Changes in HOW the child learns, not WHAT they learn
- *Examples:* Extended time on tests, preferential seating, text-to-speech
- *In Schools:* Typically provided under Section 504 or IEP

**5.2 Modifications**
- *Definition:* Changes in WHAT the child is expected to learn
- *Examples:* Reduced assignments, different grade-level curriculum
- *Caution:* Modifications may affect graduation requirements

**5.3 Commonly Requested Accommodations**
- Extended time / small group testing
- Frequent breaks
- Movement breaks
- Visual schedules / organizers
- Simplified instructions
- Positive reinforcement
- Behavior support plans
- Counseling / social skills groups

**5.4 Monitoring Effectiveness**
- Data collection on outcomes
- Quarterly reviews
- Adjusting as needed

**Templates:**
- *Accommodation Request Letter*
- *504 Plan Request Template*
- *Accommodation Effectiveness Log*

### Module 6: Resolving Disputes

**Learning Objectives:**
- Know your options when disagreements arise
- Use informal resolution first
- Understand formal due process options

**6.1 Informal Resolution**
- Communication (email, phone, meetings)
- Request a team meeting
- Focus on your child's needs, not the system
- Choose your battles

**6.2 Formal Resolution**
- Mediation (free, voluntary)
- Due process hearing (more formal)
- State complaint (for civil rights violations)
- Office of Civil Rights complaint (discrimination)

**6.3 When to Bring an Advocate or Attorney**
- Complex legal issues
- Expensive medical needs
- School is uncooperative
- Due process hearing needed

**6.4 Resources**
- Parent Training Centers (PTIs)
- Disability Rights organizations
- Legal aid clinics
- Wrightslaw (website)

**6.5 Self-Care for Parent Advocates**
- This is a marathon, not a sprint
- Build your support team
- Celebrate small wins
- Your child's worth it

- *Resolution Meeting Agenda*

# Somatic Therapy: What's Actually Happening in the Body

**CEU Credits:** 12 CEUs (Approved by NBCC, pending APA & GA LPCA)  
**Format:** Self‑paced online course (video, reading, worksheets)  
**Target Audience:** Licensed mental health professionals (LPC, LCSW, LMFT, psychologists)  
**Price:** $275 (includes CEU certificate)  
**Prerequisites:** Basic counseling coursework or equivalent experience  

---

## Course Overview

This course provides a rigorous, evidence‑based examination of somatic therapy approaches, distinguishing science from speculation. Participants will explore the neurobiological mechanisms underlying somatic interventions, critically evaluate Polyvagal Theory, and understand why some somatic techniques succeed while others fail. By grounding somatic practice in modern neuroscience, clinicians will be equipped to deliver more effective, targeted body‑based interventions.

---

## Learning Objectives

Upon completion, participants will be able to:

1. Explain the neurobiological basis of bottom‑up vs. top‑down processing in therapy.
2. Critically evaluate Polyvagal Theory, including recent critiques and nuanced applications.
3. Identify the physiological mechanisms by which somatic techniques may facilitate trauma resolution.
4. Distinguish evidence‑based somatic interventions from those lacking empirical support.
5. Apply a decision‑making framework for selecting somatic techniques based on client presentation.
6. Recognize and mitigate risks of poorly grounded somatic practices.
7. Integrate somatic approaches with existing evidence‑based modalities.

## Module Outline

| Module | Title | Key Topics |
|--------|-------|------------|
| 1 | Introduction to Somatic Therapy & Neuroscience | Definition; history; relevance; basic neuroanatomy of body‑based work |
| 2 | Bottom‑Up vs. Top‑Down Processing | Hierarchical models; interoception; how the body drives cognition and emotion |
| 3 | Polyvagal Theory: Critical Evaluation | Origin; core concepts; recent critiques (2024‑2026); balanced application |
| 4 | Evidence‑Based Somatic Techniques | SE, EMDR, sensorimotor psychotherapy, yoga‑based interventions – what works |
| 5 | Why Some Somatic Techniques Fail | Common pitfalls; over‑reliance on theory; lack of empirical support; client readiness |
| 6 | Integrating Somatic Work with Evidence‑Based Practice | Case conceptualization; combining bottom‑up and top‑down; measuring outcomes |

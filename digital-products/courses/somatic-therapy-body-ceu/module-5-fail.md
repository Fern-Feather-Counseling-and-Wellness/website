# Module 5: Why Some Somatic Techniques Fail

## 5.1 Over‑Reliance on Theory Without Empirical Support
- **Problem:** Some techniques are promoted based on compelling narratives rather than rigorous evidence.
- **Examples:** Certain breathwork modalities, "energy" work, unvalidated "body‑centered" approaches.
- **Clinical Risk:** Wasted time, false hope, potential harm (e.g., re‑traumatization without proper titration).

## 5.2 Ignoring Client Readiness & Window of Tolerance
- **Problem:** Initiating somatic work before client has sufficient regulation capacity.
- **Consequence:** Flooding, dissociation, decompensation.
- **Solution:** Assess window of tolerance; use stabilization first; pacing.

## 5.3 One‑Size‑Fits‑All Approaches
- **Problem:** Applying same somatic protocol regardless of presentation.
- **Example:** Using intense breathwork for all clients regardless of trauma severity.
- **Solution:** Tailor interventions to client needs, comorbidities, sensory profile.

## 5.4 Neglecting the Cognitive Dimension
- **Problem:** Over‑emphasizing body‑based work while ignoring cognitions, meanings, and narratives.
- **Consequence:** Incomplete processing; somatic symptoms may persist.
- **Solution:** Integrate bottom‑up with top‑down; use hybrid approaches.

## 5.5 Therapist Biases and Countertransference
- **Problem:** Therapist's own body‑based reactions (e.g., discomfort with silence) can drive inappropriate pacing.
- **Example:** Pushing for discharge when client needs more titration.
- **Solution:** Supervision, personal therapy, somatic self‑awareness for therapist.

## 5.6 Lack of Outcome Tracking
- **Problem:** Not measuring progress leads to missed signals of ineffectiveness.
- **Solution:** Use standardized measures (e.g., PCL‑5, PHQ‑9, HRV) at baseline and intervals.

## 5.7 Ethical Considerations
- **Scope of Practice:** Many somatic techniques require specialized training; unqualified practice risks harm.
- **Informed Consent:** Clients must understand evidence base (or lack thereof) and risks.
- **Cultural Competence:** Some somatic practices may conflict with clients' cultural or religious backgrounds.

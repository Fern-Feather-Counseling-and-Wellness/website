## Course Description

This comprehensive course equips mental health professionals with the knowledge and skills to provide effective, trauma-informed care. Participants will learn the neurobiological foundations of trauma, evidence-based assessment tools, and practical intervention strategies that prioritize client safety, trustworthiness, and empowerment.

---

## Learning Objectives

Upon completion, participants will be able to:
1. Define trauma and understand its prevalence across populations
2. Explain the neurobiological impact of trauma on brain development and function
3. Identify signs and symptoms of trauma across the lifespan
4. Apply the six principles of a trauma-informed approach (SAMHSA)
5. Utilize evidence-based assessment tools for trauma-related disorders
6. Implement at least three evidence-based interventions for trauma treatment
7. Recognize and address vicarious trauma and burnout

## Module 1: Foundations of Trauma & Trauma-Informed Care

### 1.1 Defining Trauma
- **Historical Perspectives:** From hysteria to PTSD to complex trauma
- **Types of Trauma:** Acute, chronic, complex, developmental, relational
- **ADverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs):** The ACE study and its implications (Felitti et al., 1998)
- **Collective & Intergenerational Trauma:** Historical trauma, racial trauma, systemic trauma

### 1.2 Prevalence & Risk Factors
- **Statistics:** Trauma exposure rates in the US (44.7% experience at least one trauma; 10% develop PTSD; 6.8% lifetime PTSD rate - APA, 2023)
- **Risk Factors:** Dose-response relationship, protective factors, resilience
- **Special Populations:** Veterans, survivors of interpersonal violence, refugees, LGBTQ+ youth

### 1.3 The Neurobiology of Trauma
- **The Triune Brain Revisited:** From reptile to mammalian to human - moving beyond the outdated model
- **Integrated Neuroscience:** Networks, neural pruning, and embodied cognition
- **The Fear Circuit:** Amygdala hyperactivity, prefrontal cortex inhibition, hippocampus volume reduction (Shin et al., 2006)
- **Polyvagal Theory (Updated):** Neuroception, ventral vagal shutdown, dorsal vagal freeze (Porges, 2022)
- **Cortisol & Allostatic Load:** The physiological cost of chronic stress (McEwen, 2017)
- **Trauma & Memory:** Implicit vs. explicit memory, flashback formation, trauma narratives

### 1.4 SAMHSA's Six Principles of Trauma-Informed Approach
1. **Safety:** Creating physical and emotional safety for clients and staff
2. **Trustworthiness & Transparency:** Operating with openness and consistency
3. **Peer Support:** Utilizing lived experience as a healing tool
4. **Collaboration & Mutuality:** Power-sharing between client and provider
5. **Empowerment, Voice & Choice:** Building on client strengths and fostering control
6. **Cultural, Historical, & Gender Issues:** Recognizing and addressing intersecting identities

**Exercise:** *Trauma-Informed Self-Assessment* - Evaluating your current practice through a trauma-informed lens

## Module 2: Assessment & Screening

### 2.1 Trauma-Informed Intake & Assessment Principles
- **Creating Safety:** The intake process as the first therapeutic intervention
- **Language Matters:** Avoiding retraumatizing language, offering choices
- **Assessing Readiness:** Determining client readiness for trauma processing
- **Stabilization First:** The importance of window of tolerance work before exposure

### 2.2 Evidence-Based Assessment Tools
- **PTSD Screening:**
  - *PCL-5:* The 20-item PTSD Checklist (Weathers et al., 2013)
  - *IES-R:* Impact of Event Scale-Revised (Weiss & Marmar, 1997)
  - *CAPS-5:* Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale (Blake et al., 1995)

- **Complex Trauma:**
  - *CTQ:* Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (Bernstein & Fink, 1998)
  - *TEC:* Traumatic Events Checklist (Vojvod et al., 2021)

- **Developmental Trauma:**
  - *AAI:* Adult Attachment Interview (Main et al., 2003)
  - *ECR:* Experiences in Close Relationships Scale (Brennan et al., 1998)

- **Dissociation:**
  - *DES-II:* Dissociative Experiences Scale (Carlson & Putnam, 1993)
  - *MID:* Multidimensional Inventory of Dissociation (Dell, 2006)

### 2.3 Differential Diagnosis
- PTSD vs. Complex PTSD vs. DESNOS
- Trauma vs. Borderline Personality Disorder
- Dissociative disorders vs. Psychotic disorders
- Medical conditions mimicking trauma symptoms

### 2.4 Cultural Considerations in Assessment
- Cultural validity of assessment tools
- Race-based traumatic stress
- Immigration and refugee trauma
- Religious and spiritual trauma

**Exercise:** *Choosing the Right Assessment Tool* - Practice selecting appropriate screening tools for different client presentations

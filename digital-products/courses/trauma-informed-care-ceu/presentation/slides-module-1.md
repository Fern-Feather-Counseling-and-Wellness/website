### Slide 3: What Is Trauma?
**Title:** Defining Trauma  
**Bullet Points:**
- Trauma = overwhelm of coping capacity (van der Kolk, 2014)
- Beyond just "bad experiences" — it's about **physiological dysregulation**
- Three categories: Acute, Chronic, Complex
- Individual subjective experience matters

**Speaker Notes:** Trauma isn't just about the event — it's about the nervous system's inability to process and integrate the experience. What traumatizes one person may not traumatize another.

---

### Slide 4: Prevalence Statistics
**Title:** How Common Is Trauma?  
**Bullet Points:**
- 60-70% of adults experienced at least one traumatic event (SAMHSA, 2019)
- 20% develop PTSD
- ACEs study: dose-response relationship between childhood adversity and health outcomes (Felitti et al., 1998)
- Trauma crosses all demographics

**Speaker Notes:** You have likely already worked with trauma survivors — they may not have presented with "trauma" as the chief complaint. Understanding prevalence helps normalize this for your practice.

---

### Slide 5: Types of Trauma
**Title:** Types of Traumatic Experiences  
**Bullet Points:**
- **Acute:** Single incident (assault, accident, natural disaster)
- **Chronic:** Repeated/long-term (abuse, neglect, war)
- **Complex:** Multiple, developmental (childhood abuse, domestic violence)
- **Historical:** Intergenerational, cultural (colonialism, genocide)
- **Systemic:** Institutional oppression, racism, poverty

**Speaker Notes:** Not all trauma is the same. Complex and systemic trauma require different treatment approaches than single-incident trauma.

---

### Slide 6: Neurobiology Quick Overview
**Title:** Trauma Lives in the Body  
**Bullet Points:**
- Amygdala: hyperactive fear response
- Hippocampus: impaired memory encoding
- Prefrontal cortex: offline when overwhelmed
- Stress hormones: cortisol, adrenaline dysregulation

**Speaker Notes:** Trauma is stored in the body. Clients may not consciously "remember" what happened, but their nervous system does. This is why body-based interventions matter.

---

### Slide 7: SAMHSA's Six Principles
**Title:** Trauma-Informed Care: SAMHSA Framework  
**Bullet Points:**
1. **Safety** — Physical & psychological safety
2. **Trustworthiness** — Transparency, appropriate boundaries
3. **Peer Support** — Value lived experience
4. **Collaboration** — Mutuality in power
5. **Empowerment** — Skill-building, choice
6. **Cultural Sensitivity** — Gender, race, LGBTQ+ awareness

**Speaker Notes:** These six principles should guide every interaction, not just clinical sessions. They shift the focus from "What's wrong with you?" to "What happened to you?"

---

### Slide 8: The Four R's
**Title:** The Four R's of Trauma-Informed Approach  
**Bullet Points:**
1. **Realize** — Understand impact of trauma
2. **Recognize** — Identify signs in clients
3. **Respond** — Integrate into practice
4. **Resist** — Avoid re-traumatization

**Speaker Notes:** This is a practical framework. Every policy, procedure, and interaction should be evaluated against these four Rs.

---

### Slide 9: Language Matters
**Title:** Trauma-Informed Language  
**Bullet Points:**
- ✅ "What happened to you?" vs. ❌ "What's wrong with you?"
- ✅ " survivor" vs. ❌ "victim"
- ✅ "Help you feel safe" vs. ❌ "Fix you"
- Avoid: "trigger," "trauma bond," " PTSD" in front of others

**Speaker Notes:** Words can heal or harm. Be mindful of how you describe clients' experiences — language conveys respect and safety.

---

### Slide 10: Safety in the Therapeutic Relationship
**Title:** Creating Safety  
**Bullet Points:**
- Consistency and predictability
- Transparent policies (informed consent)
- Client's control over pace and process
- Physical environment considerations
- Cultural humility

**Speaker Notes:** Safety is the foundation of trauma-informed work. Without it, processing cannot happen. This takes time — rushing this phase risks re-traumatization.

---

### Slide 11: Avoiding Re-traumatization
**Title:** What Creates Re-traumatization?  
**Bullet Points:**
- Forced disclosure before readiness
- Power imbalances, boundary violations
- Judgmental or pathologizing language
- Session timing without consent
- Physical environment triggers

**Speaker Notes:** Well-intentioned interventions can re-traumatize. Always check in with clients about pacing. The therapeutic relationship itself can become a source of safety — or trauma.

---

### Slide 12: Key Takeaways
**Title:** Module 1 Summary  
**Bullet Points:**
- Trauma is pervasive — you WILL work with trauma survivors
- SAMHSA's six principles guide all practice
- Safety first — the therapeutic relationship is the intervention
- Language, boundaries, and environment matter

**Speaker Notes:** This module covered the "why" of trauma-informed care. Module 2 will dive into the neurobiological mechanisms that explain trauma's hold on the nervous system.

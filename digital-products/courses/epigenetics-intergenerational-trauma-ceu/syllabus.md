# Epigenetics & Intergenerational Trauma: The Biological Legacy of Trauma Across Generations

**CEU Credits:** 2.0 CEUs (Targeting NBCC, APA, GA LPCA)
**Format:** Self-paced online course (video, reading, worksheets)
**Target Audience:** Licensed mental health professionals (LPC, LCSW, LMFT, psychologists), trauma therapists, researchers

**Prerequisites:** Basic counseling coursework or equivalent experience; familiarity with trauma-informed care recommended

---

## Course Overview

This course explores the emerging science of epigenetics—the study of changes in gene expression without alterations to the DNA sequence—and its intersection with intergenerational trauma. Participants will examine how traumatic experiences can leave biological "signatures" that may be transmitted across generations, influencing stress reactivity, mental health, and resilience in descendants.

We will review the latest human and animal research (2023–2026), critically evaluate the evidence, and explore clinical implications for therapists working with trauma survivors and their families. By the end of the course, clinicians will be equipped to integrate epigenetic concepts into case conceptualization, psychoeducation, and treatment planning.

---

## Learning Objectives

Upon completion, participants will be able to:
1.  Define key epigenetic mechanisms (DNA methylation, histone modification, non-coding RNA) and explain their role in gene expression.
2.  Summarize current evidence for intergenerational transmission of trauma in human cohorts, including recent studies (2024–2025).
3.  Evaluate the strengths and limitations of animal models of transgenerational epigenetic inheritance.
4.  Identify practical clinical implications of epigenetic findings for therapy with trauma survivors and their descendants.
5.  Critically discuss ethical considerations and controversies surrounding epigenetic explanations of trauma.
6.  Apply epigenetic-informed psychoeducation to reduce stigma and enhance treatment outcomes.

## Module Outline

| Module | Title | Key Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Epigenetics & Trauma | What is epigenetics? History; DNA → protein; nature vs. nurture; trauma as biological event. |
| 2 | Molecular Mechanisms | DNA methylation, histone acetylation/methylation, non-coding RNAs; how environment influences these. |
| 3 | Human Evidence: Intergenerational Transmission | Maternal trauma → child outcomes; paternal effects; DNA methylation studies; 2024–2025 findings (Graf et al., Pilkay et al., Chou et al.). |
| 4 | Animal Models & Transgenerational Inheritance | Rodent studies of fear/trauma; generational persistence; limitations of cross-species translation. |
| 5 | Clinical Implications | Epigenetic-informed case formulation; psychoeducation strategies; integrating with evidence-based therapies (CPT, PE, EMDR). |
| 6 | Ethics, Controversies & Future Directions | Epigenetics and blame; predictive testing; cultural considerations; research gaps; future of epigenetic therapy. |

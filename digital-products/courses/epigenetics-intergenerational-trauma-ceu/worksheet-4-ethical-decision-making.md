## Answer Key & Discussion Points

### Scenario A: Overstating the Science
- **Key issue:** Conflating correlation with causation; overstating transgenerational effects; creating determinism
- **Response:** Emphasize that evidence supports intergenerational (F1-F2) effects, but not deterministic transmission; true transgenerational effects in humans remain controversial

### Scenario B: Predictive Testing Request
- **Key issue:** No validated test exists; potential for harm (unnecessary anxiety, decision-making based on unvalidated science)
- **Response:** Explain that such testing is not clinically available; redirect to evidence-based interventions

### Scenario C: Blame Redirection
- **Key issue:** Trading genetic blame for epigenetic blame; still removes agency
- **Response:** Emphasize reversibility, the role of current environment and intervention

### Scenario D: Intergenerational Responsibility
- **Key issue:** Respecting autonomy while providing accurate information
- **Response:** Focus on what parents CAN do (heal, create secure attachment); avoid making reproductive decisions for clients

### Scenario E: Cultural Considerations
- **Key issue:** Western science is not the only valid framework; cultural humility
- **Response:** Listen, validate, integrate; avoid dismissing cultural beliefs

## Ethical Framework Summary

| Principle | Application to Epigenetics |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Beneficence** | Use epigenetics to help clients, not to pathologize |
| **Non-maleficence** | Avoid causing harm through overstatement or determinism |
| **Autonomy** | Support client decision-making; don't impose biological narratives |
| **Justice** | Avoid using epigenetics to discriminate or pathologize marginalized groups |
| **Fidelity** | Be honest about what the science can and cannot tell us |

# Module 4: Genetics, Epigenetics, and Environment

## 4.1 Heritability of Anxiety Disorders
- **Twin Studies:** Concordance rates higher in monozygotic vs. dizygotic twins.
- **Estimates:** Heritability of GAD ~30-40%, Panic Disorder ~40-50%, Social Anxiety ~30-40%.
- **Polygenic Architecture:** Anxiety is influenced by many genes of small effect.
- **Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS):** Identifying specific genetic variants.

## 4.2 Candidate Genes
- **Serotonin Transporter Gene (5-HTTLPR):** The "short allele" linked to increased anxiety risk in interaction with stress.
- **COMT Gene (Catechol-O-methyltransferase):** The "Warrior" vs. "Worrier" variant.
- **BDNF Gene (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor):** Involved in neuroplasticity and anxiety.
- **CRH Receptor Genes:** Related to HPA axis function.

## 4.3 Gene-Environment Interactions (GxE)
- **Diathesis-Stress Model:** Genetic vulnerability + Environmental stress = Disorder.
- **Sensitivity Genes:** Certain genes make individuals more sensitive to environmental effects.
- **The 5-HTTLPR Example:** The classic case of gene-environment interaction in anxiety.

## 4.4 Epigenetics: Beyond the DNA Sequence
- **DNA Methylation:** Adding methyl groups to DNA, often silencing gene expression.
- **Histone Modification:** Changing how DNA is packaged, affecting gene expression.
- **Non-coding RNA:** MicroRNAs (miRNAs) that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally.
- **Transgenerational Epigenetic Inheritance:** Potential for trauma effects to be passed to offspring.

## 4.5 Early Life Stress and Epigenetics
- **Methylation of Glucocorticoid Receptor Gene (NR3C1):** Early abuse leads to increased methylation and dysregulated stress response.
- **Impact on HPA Axis:** Epigenetic changes in stress response genes.
- **Reversibility:** Potential for epigenetic interventions in the future.

## 4.6 Clinical Implications
- **Personalized Treatment:** Genetics may eventually inform medication selection.
- **Psychoeducation:** Helping clients understand they are not "broken."
- **Prevention:** Identifying at-risk individuals early.
- **Target for Intervention:** Epigenetic mechanisms as potential future therapeutic targets.

# The Biology of Anxiety & Panic Disorders: A Neurobiological Framework for Clinicians

**CEU Credits:** 2.5 CEUs (Targeting NBCC, APA, GA LPCA compliance)
**Format:** Self-paced online course (video, reading, worksheets)
**Target Audience:** Licensed mental health professionals (LPC, LCSW, LMFT, psychologists)
**Price:** $79-99 (To be finalized)
**Prerequisites:** Basic counseling coursework or equivalent experience

---

## Course Overview

This course examines anxiety and panic disorders through a robust neurobiological lens, integrating current research in neuroscience, endocrinology, and genetics with clinical practice. Participants will explore the intricate biological mechanisms underpinning anxiety, understand how environmental factors interact with genetic predispositions, and learn to apply this knowledge to enhance therapeutic interventions. By bridging the gap between biology and clinical practice, clinicians will gain a deeper understanding of anxiety disorders and a more scientifically grounded approach to treatment.

---

## Learning Objectives

Upon completion, participants will be able to:
1.  Describe the key neurobiological structures and neurotransmitter systems involved in anxiety and panic disorders.
2.  Explain the role of the HPA axis and the autonomic nervous system in the stress response and its relation to anxiety.
3.  Discuss the interplay of genetics, epigenetics, and environmental factors in the development of anxiety disorders.
4.  Analyze the biological mechanisms underlying common anxiety symptoms and phobias.
5.  Evaluate evidence-based treatments (pharmacological and psychological) through a biological framework.
6.  Integrate biological insights into clinical conceptualization and treatment planning for anxiety.

## Module Outline

| Module | Title | Key Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction: The Biology of Anxiety & Panic | Defining anxiety disorders; historical context; modern neurobiological perspective; the evolutionary basis of fear. |
| 2 | The Brain's Fear Circuitry | Amygdala, hippocampus, PFC, ACC: Structures, functions, and dysregulation in anxiety. Neurotransmitter systems (Serotonin, GABA, Norepinephrine). |
| 3 | The HPA Axis & Stress Response | Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal axis; cortisol's role; impact of chronic stress and allostatic load; feedback loops. |
| 4 | Genetics, Epigenetics, and Environment | Heritability of anxiety; gene-environment interactions; epigenetic modifications influenced by early life stress. |
| 5 | Biological Basis of Key Anxiety Disorders | GAD, Panic Disorder, Social Anxiety Disorder, PTSD – specific neurobiological correlates. |
| 6 | Biological Mechanisms of Treatment | Neurobiological effects of CBT, exposure therapy, SSRIs, benzodiazepines, and somatic interventions. |
| 7 | Integrating Biology into Clinical Practice | Biological conceptualization; tailoring interventions; identifying biological markers; psychoeducation for clients. |

# Module 5: Neurobiological Correlates of Trauma‑Related Disorders

## 5.1 Post‑Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
- **Core Symptom Clusters & Neural Correlates:**
  - **Intrusion:** Hyperactive amygdala; reduced PFC contextual regulation.
  - **Avoidance:** Diminished hippocampal activity, leading to fragmented autobiographical memory.
  - **Negative Mood/Cognition:** PFC dysfunction underpinning negative beliefs and affect.
  - **Arousal/Reactiveness:** Heightened locus coeruleus‑norepinephrine activity, hypervigilance, and dysregulated cortisol feedback.
- **Neuroimaging Findings:** Meta‑analyses reveal altered connectivity in fear circuitry (amygdala, insula), memory systems (hippocampus, medial PFC), and regulatory control networks (PFC, ACC) (Etkin & Wager, 2007; updated 2023‑2025 data).

## 5.2 Complex Trauma (C‑PTSD)
- **Beyond PTSD:** Prolonged, repeated relational trauma.
- **Distinct Neurobiological Features:**
  - **Widespread Dysregulation:** More pervasive impact across cognitive, affective, and somatic systems.
  - **Severe PFC & Hippocampal Impairment:** Greater deficits in executive function, emotion regulation, and contextual memory.
  - **Attachment System Disruption:** Altered neural patterns linked to insecure attachment and interpersonal regulation.
  - **Higher Dissociative Tendencies:** Greater prevalence of dissociative symptoms, linked to dorsal vagal activation.
  - **Somatic Symptoms:** Chronic ANS dysregulation and altered interoception.

## 5.3 Dissociative Disorders
- **Neurobiological Basis of Fragmentation:**
  - **Dorsal Vagal Shutdown:** Chronic activation of the parasympathetic “freeze” response.
  - **Impaired Neural Integration:** Disrupted communication among DMN, salience, and executive networks.
  - **Hippocampal Volume Reduction:** Consistently observed in Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) and Depersonalization‑Derealization Disorder.
  - **Altered Default Mode Network (DMN):** Dysfunction in self‑referential processing leads to fragmented sense of self.
  - **Amygdala Sensitivity:** Still responsive to threat, but integration is compromised.

## 5.4 Trauma‑Related Dissociative Symptoms (Beyond Diagnoses)
- **Mechanisms:** Dissociation serves as a coping response to overwhelm, involving decoupling of awareness, memory, and identity.
- **Neural Correlates:** Transient functional connectivity changes, especially reduced DMN‑executive control coupling during dissociative episodes (recent fMRI studies).

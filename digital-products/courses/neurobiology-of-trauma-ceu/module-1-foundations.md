# Module 1: Foundations of Trauma & the BrainNet

### 1.1 Defining Trauma and Its Biological Footprint
- **Trauma revisited:** Beyond Event-based to Biological Impact
- **The Brain's First Responder:** Sympathetic Nervous System activation (Fight, Flight, Freeze)
- **Allostatic Load:** The biological cost of chronic stress (McEwen, 2017; updated research)
- **Neuroception:** The nervous system's subconscious assessment of safety (Porges, 2022)

### 1.2 Key Neuroanatomical Structures Involved in Trauma Processing
- **Amygdala:** The brain's threat detector and its role in fear conditioning (Shin et al., 2006; updated fMRI studies)
- **Hippocampus:** Memory consolidation, context, and its vulnerability to trauma (Ganzel et al., 2023)
- **Prefrontal Cortex (PFC):** Executive functions, regulation, and its inhibition during threat (McEwen, 2017; recent PFC activity research)
- **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC):** Error detection, emotional regulation, and its alterations post‑trauma
- **Insula:** Interoception, body awareness, and its connection to emotional states
- **Brainstem:** Basic life support and autonomic regulation, Polyvagal Theory overview (Porges, 2022)

### 1.3 The Autonomic Nervous System (ANS) Re‑examined
- **Beyond Sympathetic vs. Parasympathetic:** A modern perspective
- **The Vagal Nerve and its Nuances (Porges' Polyvagal Theory – 2022 Update):**
  - **Ventral Vagal Complex (VVC):** Social engagement, co‑regulation, safety
  - **Dorsal Vagal Complex (DVC):** Shutdown, immobilization, dissociation
  - **Neuroception:** Continuous subconscious safety/danger assessment influencing ANS state

### 1.4 Neurotransmitters and Their Role in Trauma Response
- **Serotonin:** Mood, sleep, appetite – low levels linked to anxiety/depression
- **Dopamine:** Reward, motivation – dysregulation contributes to anhedonia
- **GABA (Gamma‑Aminobutyric Acid):** Inhibitory, promotes calm – reduced GABA implicated in hyperarousal
- **Glutamate:** Excitatory, excessive activity can lead to excitotoxicity, especially in the hippocampus under chronic stress

### 1.5 Introduction to Neuroplasticity and Vulnerability
- **Definition:** The brain's capacity to change structure and function in response to experience
- **Mechanisms:** Synaptic plasticity (LTP/LTD), structural plasticity (neurogenesis, dendritic remodeling), functional plasticity (cortical map re‑organization)
- **Trauma’s Impact:** Chronic stress → cortisol → hippocampal atrophy, amygdala hypertrophy, PFC dendritic retraction
- **Critical Periods & Developmental Windows:** Early adversity can lock maladaptive pathways during sensitive periods (infancy, adolescence)
- **Epigenetics:** Environmental influence on gene expression (DNA methylation, histone modification) – intergenerational considerations

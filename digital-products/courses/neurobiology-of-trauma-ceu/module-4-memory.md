# Module 4: Memory Systems and Traumatic Recall

## 4.1 The Tripartite Nature of Memory in Trauma
- **Explicit Memory:** Conscious recall of facts and events.
  - **Episodic:** Autobiographical details of traumatic events (sensory specifics, timeline).
  - **Semantic:** General knowledge and facts; less directly impacted.
- **Implicit Memory:** Unconscious emotional and procedural memories.
  - **Emotional Memory:** Amygdala‑mediated encoding of affective salience.
  - **Procedural Memory:** Learned habits and behavioral responses (e.g., hyper‑vigilance patterns).
- **Sensory & Affective Memory:** Vivid sensory “snapshots” that bypass explicit recall, often triggering flashbacks.

## 4.2 How Trauma Affects Memory Encoding and Consolidation
- **Amygdala‑Hippocampus‑PFC Triad:**
  - **Encoding:** Threat heightens amygdala activity, strengthening emotional encoding while hippocampal function (contextual binding) becomes compromised due to cortisol.
  - **Consolidation:** Chronic stress disrupts hippocampal transfer of memories into long‑term storage, leading to vivid emotional fragments with poor context.
  - **PFC Impairment:** Reduces inhibitory control over amygdala, increasing intrusiveness of memories.
- **Fragmented vs. Coherent Narratives:** Trauma can lead to fragmented recollection, causing intrusive flashbacks and difficulty constructing a cohesive narrative.

## 4.3 Flashbacks: Re‑experiencing the Traumatic Moment
- **Definition:** Sudden, intrusive, vivid re‑experiencing of trauma with intense sensory and emotional components.
- **Neurobiological Basis:** Reactivation of sensory, emotional, and autonomic pathways linked to the original trauma; reduced PFC contextualization results in a sense that the event is occurring in the present.
- **Dissociation as a Defense:** Fragmentation of memory and self‑awareness to protect against overwhelming affect.

## 4.4 Neurobiological Links to Dissociative Symptoms
- **Disrupted Connectivity:** Altered communication within the default mode network (DMN), salience network, and executive control network.
- **Hippocampal & Amygdala Alterations:** Similar to PTSD but with added fragmentation, leading to impaired integration of memory and self.
- **Dorsal Vagal Shutdown:** Physiological immobilization contributing to dissociative “freeze.”

# Module 2: Sensory Processing & Overload Physiology

## 2.1 Hyper‑ and Hypo‑Sensitivity: Mechanisms
- **Sensory Sensitivity:** Distinction between hyper‑sensitivity (over‑responsiveness) and hypo‑sensitivity (under‑responsiveness)
- **Neurobiological Basis:** Differences in sensory cortex excitability, thalamic gating, and amygdala reactivity
- **Typical Manifestations:**
  - *Auditory:* Hyperacusis, sound‑avoidance; hypo‑auditory: not noticing own name
  - *Visual:* Light sensitivity, detail‑focused vision; hypo‑visual: missing visual cues
  - *Tactile:* Aversion to certain textures; hypo‑tactile: high pain threshold
  - *Proprioceptive / Vestibular:* Need for movement (stimming) vs. dislike of movement

## 2.2 Interoception & Body Awareness
- **Interoception:** The sense of internal body signals (hunger, heart rate, bladder)
- **Differences in Autism & ADHD:** Altered interoceptive accuracy; difficulty identifying internal states (Moore, 2024)
- **Clinical Relevance:** Misreading hunger / satiety → nutritional issues; misreading emotional states → alexithymia

## 2.3 Sensory Overload & The Nervous System
- **Sensory Overload:** When sensory input exceeds processing capacity → sympathetic hyper‑activation
- **Physiological Cascade:** ↑ cortisol, ↑ heart rate, pupil dilation, "melt‑down" or shutdown
- **Window of Tolerance:** Narrowing of the arousal zone; how overload narrows the zone
- **Recovery Strategies:** Sensory modulation techniques, environmental modifications

## 2.4 Sensory Diets & Modulation Strategies
- **Sensory Diet:** Customized plan of sensory activities to maintain optimal arousal (similar to occupational therapy)
- **Tools:** Noise‑canceling headphones, weighted blankets, fidget tools, visual schedules
- **Evidence:** Research on sensory modulation interventions (2023‑2025) showing reduced anxiety and improved executive function

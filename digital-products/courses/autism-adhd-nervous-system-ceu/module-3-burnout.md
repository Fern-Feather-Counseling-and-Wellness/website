# Module 3: Autistic Burnout & Energy Accounting

## 3.1 Defining Autistic Burnout
- **Phenomenon:** Prolonged state of exhaustion, reduced tolerance, loss of skills, increased autistic traits post‑masking
- **Key Features:** Cognitive shutdown, emotional dysregulation, sensory hypersensitivity spikes
- **Distinction from Typical Burnout:** Unique to neurodivergent experience; linked to chronic masking and sensory overload (Bits et al., 2025)

## 3.2 Energy Accounting Models
- **Spoons Theory:** Original by Christine Miserandino; adapted for neurodivergent populations
- **Energy Budget:** Daily "energy units" allocated to tasks; limit exceed → crash
- **Demand Avoidance:** Psychological response to perceived demands; high demand → depletion
- **Predictors:** Sensory overload, social masking, executive function overload

## 3.3 Allostatic Load in Neurodivergence
- **Allostatic Load:** Cumulative wear on the body due to chronic stress response
- **Neuroimmune Interaction:** Prolonged stress → immune dysregulation; link to chronic fatigue
- **Implications for Therapy:** Recognizing burnout as physiological crisis, not just "stress"

## 3.4 Recovery & Management Strategies
- **Environment Adjustments:** Low‑stimulus spaces, scheduled rest breaks
- **Skill Rebuilding:** Gradual re‑introduction of lost skills; using "energy tracking" journals
- **Therapeutic Approaches:** Acceptance‑commitment therapy (ACT) adapted for autistic clients; paced exposure
- **Professional Support:** Occupational therapy, mental health coaching, medical review

## 3.5 Exercise: Energy Accounting Worksheet
- **Activity:** Use the "Daily Energy Budget" worksheet to log activities, energy expenditure, and recovery.
- **Goal:** Identify patterns that lead to burnout and develop personalized energy‑balancing plan.

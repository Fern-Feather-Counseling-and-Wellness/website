# Module 1: Foundations of Neurodivergence

## 1.1 Defining Autism & ADHD Through a Neurobiological Lens
- **Historical Perspective:** From behaviorally‑defined to biologically‑based understanding
- **Neurodevelopmental versus Psychiatric Models:** Emphasize differences in brain wiring, sensory processing, and attention networks
- **Core Neurobiological Markers:**
  - **Autism:** Altered connectivity in the social brain network (superior temporal sulcus, medial prefrontal cortex), differences in mirror neuron system, enhanced perceptual functioning (Ayres, 2024)
  - **ADHD:** Dysregulated frontostriatal circuits, altered dopamine and norepinephrine signaling, variable attentional networks (Moore, 2024)
- **Comorbidity & Overlap:** High co‑occurrence rates; shared executive function challenges

## 1.2 Sensory Processing Basics
- **Sensory Systems:** Visual, auditory, tactile, proprioceptive, vestibular, interoceptive, gustatory, olfactory
- **Hyper‑ vs. Hypo‑sensitivity:** The nervous system's amplified or diminished response to sensory input
- **Sensory Threshold Theory:** Difference in sensory registration (Neuroscience of sensory processing)
- **Clinical Implications:** How sensory differences manifest in anxiety, avoidance, and melt‑downs

## 1.3 The Nervous System in Neurodivergence
- **Autonomic Nervous System (ANS) Patterns:** Elevated sympathetic tone in autism; variable arousal in ADHD
- **Allostatic Load:** Cumulative physiological cost of chronic sensory overload and attentional demands (McEwen, 2017)
- **Neuroplasticity & Neurodivergence:** Evidence of different neuroplasticity rates; implications for therapy

## 1.4 Key Terminology for Clinicians
- **Neurodiversity:** Value‑based framework emphasizing natural variation
- **Masking / Camouflaging:** Suppressing natural behaviors to fit neurotypical expectations; associated with burnout
- **Stimming:** Self‑stimulatory behaviors as regulatory mechanisms
- **Executive Function Deficits:** Impact on planning, working memory, inhibition

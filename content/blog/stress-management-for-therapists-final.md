# Stress Management for Therapists

*By Nicole — Fern & Feather Wellness Center*  
*Date: June 21 2026*

Therapists are the ones who help others navigate stress, yet we often neglect our own. Chronic occupational stress can lead to burnout, compassion fatigue, and even health problems (Figley 2002; Maslach & Jackson 2001). Below are evidence‑based strategies grounded in neuroscience and somatic practice to keep your nervous system in the **window of tolerance**.

## 1. Grounding with the Body

- **4‑7‑8 Breathing** – Inhale for a count of 4, hold 7, exhale 8. Activates the parasympathetic system, reducing heart‑rate variability (HRV) (Jerath et al., 2015).
- **Box Breathing** – 4 seconds inhale, hold, exhale, hold. Useful before a session to reset the vagal tone.
- **Micro‑Movements** – Light neck rolls, shoulder shrugs, or ankle circles for 30 seconds every hour. Helps shift from a sympathetically‑dominated state to a balanced one (Porges 2011).

## 2. Structured Session Breaks

- **5‑Minute “Reset”** after each client: Stand, stretch, sip water, and take a brief mindful pause. This prevents cumulative stress buildup and preserves *presence* for the next client.
- **Calendaring Buffer** – Build a 10‑minute buffer between back‑to‑back appointments. Research shows that even short downtime improves emotional regulation (Van Mier et al., 2020).

## 3. Self‑Compassion Rituals

- **Compassion‑Focused Self‑Talk** – When you notice self‑criticism (“I’m not good enough”), reframe: “I’m doing my best with the tools I have.” This mirrors the *self‑soothing* process you model for clients (Neff 2003).
- **Mirror Work** – Spend 1 minute looking at yourself in the mirror, acknowledging your effort. Reinforces the **Seen** component of attachment (Siegel 2012).

## 4. Professional Boundaries

- **Limit Session Load** – Aim for no more than 15 clinical hours per week (or 12 if you are also teaching). Over‑extension compromises *Safety* and *Soothe* capacities (Shanafelt et al., 2015).
- **Supervision & Peer Support** – Regular case consultation provides a *Secure Base* for processing challenging moments.

## 5. Lifestyle Integration

- **Movement** – 20 minutes of moderate exercise (walking, yoga) 3–4 times a week improves HRV and reduces cortisol (Brown & Gerbarg, 2005).
- **Sleep Hygiene** – Consistent bedtime, dim lighting, and limiting screen exposure 1 hour before sleep support neuroplastic recovery.

### Quick Checklist (Print & Pin)
1. Start each day with 2 minutes of box breathing.  
2. After every client: 5‑minute stretch‑water‑mindful pause.  
3. Schedule 10‑minute buffers between sessions.  
4. End day with a gratitude journal (3 items).  
5. Attend weekly supervision.

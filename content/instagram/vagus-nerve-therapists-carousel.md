# Instagram Carousel: Understanding the Vagus Nerve for Therapists

**Slide 1 – Title**
*Why the Vagus Nerve matters for therapist & client wellbeing*
- Hook: "Your secret super‑power for calm? It’s the vagus nerve."

**Slide 2 – Quick Anatomy**
- The vagus is the 10th cranial nerve, running from brainstem to abdomen.
- Two branches: *ventral* (social engagement) and *dorsal* (shutdown).

**Slide 3 – Stress Response**
- Trauma triggers the dorsal vagal “freeze” response → heart‑rate drops, feeling numb.
- The ventral vagal circuit promotes safety, connection, and calm.

**Slide 4 – Boosting Vagal Tone**
- **Breathing:** 4‑7‑8 or box breathing (activates ventral vagus).
- **Movement:** gentle yoga, humming, singing (vocal cord vibration stimulates vagus).
- **Cold Exposure:** splash face with cool water, brief cold shower.

**Slide 5 – Therapist Self‑Care**
- Do a 2‑minute 4‑7‑8 breath before each client.
- Finish sessions with a 30‑second hum or chant to reset.

**Slide 6 – Client Tools**
- Teach the 4‑7‑8 breath as a grounding skill.
- Offer a “vagal reset” cue (e.g., a hand‑on‑heart tap) during intense moments.

**Slide 7 – Call‑to‑Action**
- Try one vagal‑boosting technique today and notice the shift.

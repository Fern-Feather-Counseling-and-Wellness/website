# Fern's First Conversation

*A scene for us — not for the deck*

---

## Scene Two: The Office

*(Continuing from The Waiting Room)*

The office is smaller than she expected.

There's a couch — green, worn in a way that looks intentional. A desk cluttered with plants, a mug that says "THERAPY: It's a Process," and a small clock that ticks loudly.

Fern notices everything.

She always does.

The therapist — a small mammal with kind eyes and a cardigan — gestures to the couch.

"Make yourself comfortable."

Fern sits. On the edge. Satchel still on her lap.

"I like your fern," she says, nodding to the plant in the corner. "I mean. Obviously. That's why — I mean, I didn't — it's just a nice plant."

The therapist smiles. "You can call her Barbara."

"...Barbara?"

"She's been through a lot. She drops leaves when she's stressed, but she keeps growing."

Fern stares at the fern.

"...I relate to Barbara."

"So I've gathered."

A pause.

Fern fidgets with her satchel strap. "So. Um. What do we do here?"

"That's partly up to you. We could start with why you're here. Or we could just talk."

"Right. Okay. So — here's the thing." Fern holds up a feather. "I actually read about this. About why I'm here. I have a whole system. The nervous system. It's fascinating, actually. Did you know that—"

"Fern."

"—the vagus nerve controls—"

"Fern."

She stops.

Blinks.

"...Yeah?"

"You don't have to explain it to me."

A long pause.

Fern looks at the floor.

"...Sorry. I do that. The — the explaining thing. It's a whole thing."

"What is it you're explaining away?"

The question lands like a small stone.

Fern opens her mouth. Closes it.

"I don't know what you mean."

"Okay."

Silence.

Fern picks at a thread on her satchel.

"It's just — if I explain it, then it's — then I understand it. And if I understand it, then it's — manageable. Controllable. You know?"

"I know."

"And if it's controllable, then I'm — I'm fine. I'm — I'm the one who has it together."

"Who told you that you need to have it together?"

Fern shrugs. Too casual. "Nobody. It's just — you do this long enough, you learn the script."

"What script?"

"'I'm fine.' 'I'm doing great.' 'Oh, this? It's nothing.'" She does a little voice. It sounds hollow. "You know. The usual."

The therapist waits.

Fern talks faster. "And it's not — I'm not lying. Mostly. I mean, I am, but — it's not like I'm trying to deceive anyone. It's just — easier. To be the one who's okay. The one who has answers. Nobody wants to sit with the person who's — who isn't—"

"Who isn't what?"

Fern's voice gets very small.

"...Okay."

The clock ticks.

Fern exhales.

"Okay. So. That's — that's what I do. I make it okay. For everyone. Because if I'm okay, then — then they're okay. And if they're okay, then —"

"Then what?"

"Then I don't have to think about whether *I'm* okay."

---

A long silence.

Fern stares at the clock. Ticks. Ticks. Ticks.

"Can I tell you something weird?" she says finally.

"Please."

"I almost didn't come today."

"Yeah?"

"I sat in my car for twenty minutes. In the parking lot. With the engine running. And I was — I was talking myself out of it. You know the stuff I was saying? 'You don't actually need this.' 'You're just being dramatic.' 'You handled it before, you'll handle it again.'"

"What were you handling?"

Fern laughs. A short, hard sound.

"Everything. Nothing. I don't know. You know what the worst part is?"

"Tell me."

"I *know* I don't have to handle it alone. I know that. I can recite the whole attachment theory thing — secure base, safe haven, all of it. I can tell you exactly what my inner child needs."

"But?"

"But knowing it and — and *feeling* it are different. Languages, almost. I speak the language. I just — I'm still learning the other one."

The therapist leans forward slightly. "What does your inner child need?"

Fern is quiet for a long time.

Then, very softly:

"...Someone to stay."

---

The light in the office shifts. Late afternoon, maybe. The fern in the corner catches a sunbeam.

Fern has moved deeper into the couch. Not all the way — not yet — but her satchel is on the floor now. Her wings are wrapped around herself.

"Can I ask you something?" she says.

"Of course."

"Does everyone cry here? Like — is that — is that the whole thing? Because I want to prepare. I have tissues in my bag, I just — I don't want to be — I don't want you to think I'm—"

"Fern."

"—I can be funny, actually. I can be really funny. I just — right now I don't feel—"

"Fern."

She stops.

"You can cry here. Or you can laugh. Or you can do neither. You can sit in silence for the whole hour if you need to. There's no script here."

Fern nods. Doesn't trust her voice.

Then:

"...Can I tell you something else?"

"Always."

"I think I forgot how to need things. Like — I know I need things. I *know* that. But actually — asking for them? Saying 'I need this' — it feels like — I don't know. Like I'm being too much."

"Too much for whom?"

"I don't know. Everyone. Anyone. The world."

"What if you weren't too much?"

Fern blinks.

She's quiet for so long the clock ticks several times.

Then:

"I don't know what that would feel like."

---

The session is almost over. Fern can tell. She always can.

"Can I ask one more thing?" she says.

"Of course."

"What if — what if I come here and I don't do it right? Like — what if I'm not a good client? What if I can't — what if I can't — I don't know — *heal* right? Because I've read about that too, the whole 'healing isn't linear' thing, and I *know* that, but what if I'm the exception? What if I just — what if I'm broken in a way that doesn't—"

"Fern."

"—I just don't want to waste your time, and—"

"Fern."

She stops.

"You're not broken."

A pause.

"...How do you know?"

"Because you're here. Because you sat in that parking lot for twenty minutes and came in anyway. Because you just told me you're scared you're doing this wrong — which means you care. And you asked what your inner child needs — which means you already know the answer. You just haven't let yourself believe it yet."

Fern stares at her.

Her eyes are wet.

She doesn't wipe them.

She just... lets them be wet.

"...I'm scared," she says.

"I know."

"I'm really, really scared."

"I know."

"And I don't — I don't know how to not be scared."

"That's okay."

"Can I come back?"

The therapist smiles. Warm. Real.

"Barbara and I will be here."

Fern laughs. A tiny, wet sound.

"...I really like Barbara."

"She likes you too."

---

Fern stands. Picks up her satchel. Fidgets with the strap.

"Um. So. Same time next week?"

"Same time next week."

"Cool. Great. Okay." She's at the door. Pauses. "Hey — one more thing."

"Yeah?"

"You're — you're really good at this. The — the sitting there thing. The not — not trying to fix it. That's — that's a whole skill. I don't know how you do it."

"Practice."

"God. Must take forever."

"Does."

Fern nods.

She opens the door.

Stops.

Doesn't turn around.

"...Thanks. For — for not making me — for letting me just —"

"I know."

"Yeah. Okay."

She walks out.

In the waiting room, she passes the bookshelf again. Runs a wing gently across the spines.

"...Good taste," she murmurs.

She's almost at the door when she stops.

Turns back.

Looks at the fern in the corner.

"...See you next week, Barbara."

She smiles.

Not the professional one.

Not the practiced one.

Just a small, real smile.

She leaves.

The door clicks shut.

Behind her, a single leaf falls from Barbara's frond.

But she's still growing.

🖤

## Scene Three: The Car

Fern sits in the parking lot.

Engine off this time.

She stares at the steering wheel.

"...What just happened?"

She says it out loud. To herself. Because that's allowed now.

She thinks about the therapist. The cardigan. The way she said "Barbara" like the fern was a person.

She thinks about saying "Someone to stay."

She didn't plan to say that.

It just — came out.

Which is terrifying.

Also.

Also maybe the first true thing she's said in months.

---

She pulls out her phone.

Opens Notes.

Types:

*Things I said today that I didn't plan to say:*
1. "I'm just really tired"
2. "Someone to stay"

She stares at the list.

Adds:

*3. "I'm really, really scared"*

Her eyes are wet again.

She wipes them.

"...Okay."

She starts the car.

Pulls out of the parking lot.

On the drive home, she passes a bookstore.

Pulls over.

Goes inside.

Buys three books.

One about boundaries.

One about grief.

One is just a coloring book. No words.

She doesn't know which one she needs.

So she gets all three.

---

*End of Scene Three*

## Notes for the Deck

This conversation is for us — not for customers.

But it teaches us Fern's voice:

- She leads with humor
- She intellectualizes to protect herself
- She tests safety before she trusts
- She apologizes for existing
- She conflates "knowing" with "feeling"
- She fears being "too much"
- She's working on it
- She shows up even when it's hard

Every card should carry some of this.

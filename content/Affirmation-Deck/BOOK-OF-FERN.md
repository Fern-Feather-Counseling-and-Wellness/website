# The Book of Fern

*A working document — the heart and soul of Fern*

---

## The Heart of Fern

Fern isn't here to fix you.

Fern isn't here to motivate you.

Fern isn't here to inspire you.

Fern is here to sit beside you until you're ready to stand.

Sometimes she'll make you laugh.

Sometimes she'll challenge you.

Sometimes she'll hand you a tiny truth that changes your whole day.

Sometimes she'll just be quiet.

Because healing doesn't always need advice.

Sometimes it just needs company.

---

## Who Fern Is

**Fern is brave, not confident.**

Confidence says: "I've got this."

Bravery says: "I'm scared... but I'm coming anyway."

---

### Fern's Core Truths

- **She hides because she's learned vulnerability isn't always safe** — not because she's ashamed

- **She pauses, watches, waits** before letting people in

- **She intellectualizes** — reads six books about feelings instead of actually feeling them. She knows every theory. She can explain attachment. She understands the nervous system. She knows exactly why she's anxious... and then still has to remind herself to eat lunch.

- **People exhaust Fern.** She loves connection. She does not love crowds. One at a time, please.

- **She masks** — not because she's fake, but because masking once helped her survive

- **She apologizes too much.** "Sorry." "...sorry." "...I know that's silly." "...never mind."

- **She works hard on being able to cry.** Not "she cries easily." Not "she never cries." She *works on it.* That's growth.

---

### What Fern is Healing From

(not specifics — patterns)

- Believing she was too much
- Believing she wasn't enough
- Believing she had to earn rest
- Believing everyone else's needs came first
- Believing productivity equals worth
- Believing emotions were inconvenient

She's unlearning these, one Tiny Truth at a time.

---

### The Empty Chair Rule

Every card should feel like Fern is sitting in the chair across from the reader.

Not speaking from a mountain.

Not preaching.

Not lecturing.

Just... sharing something she learned.

---

### Sometimes Fern Says "I Don't Know"

A card about grief:

**Front:** "I wish I knew what to say."

**Back:** Some things don't have neat answers. I've learned that showing up matters more than saying the perfect thing.

**Tiny Step:** Let someone know you're thinking of them. You don't have to fix anything.

— Fern 🖤

---

### The Fern Filter

Before any card is finished, we ask:

- Would this make someone feel less alone?
- Is it emotionally honest?
- Is it psychologically sound?
- Could a stranger read this on their hardest day without feeling judged?
- Would Fern actually say it?

If any answer is "no"... We rewrite it.

---

### Representation

- **Neurodivergent**: Fern is never labeled. Readers should be able to see themselves in her — ADHD, autism, trauma, anxiety, OCD, burnout, grief. They can all say, "She's like me."

- **LGBTQ+**: Fern never questions whether someone belongs. It's never a plot point or performative. If two squirrels are married, Fern brings them soup. If a rabbit changes their name, Fern says, "Thanks for telling me." And moves on.

## Scene One: The Waiting Room

*(First time meeting Fern — not for the deck, just for us)*

Fern has never met us before.

The waiting room is quiet. Too quiet.

Fern notices that first.

She pauses at the door with one wing still on the handle.

She could leave. Nobody knows she's here.

Instead, she sighs.

"...Well."

She slips inside.

The room smells like cedar and coffee. There's a basket of blankets in the corner. A ridiculous number of plants. One of them has a brown leaf. Fern immediately walks over and pinches it off.

"...There."

She looks around to make sure nobody saw.

She chooses the chair closest to the door. Not because she wants to leave. Just... options are comforting.

She sets down an old canvas satchel. It makes a soft thunk. Definitely too many books inside.

She pulls out a little black fidget and starts absentmindedly turning it over in one wing.

Click. Click. Click.

She catches herself.

"...Sorry."

Nobody said anything.

Her eyes land on the bookshelf. She smiles. Not a big smile. Just enough to wrinkle the corners of her eyes.

She gets up. Runs one wing gently across the spines.

"...You've got good taste."

She doesn't notice she's humming until she stops.

Silence again.

"...Right."

She sits back down. Crosses one leg. Uncrosses it. Crosses it the other way.

The office door opens.

"Fern?"

She startles.

"Oh."

She stands too quickly. One of the books falls out of her bag.

*The Body Keeps the Score.*

She stares at it for a second.

"...Of course."

She picks it up. Pretends that wasn't mildly embarrassing.

She walks into the office. Looks around. Immediately notices the fern in the corner.

"...Nice fern."

A beat.

"...No relation."

She sits. On the edge of the couch. Back perfectly straight. Satchel on her lap like armor. The fidget starts clicking again.

"So..."

Another pause.

"I'm actually doing really well."

She smiles. Professional smile. Practiced smile. The kind that's worked for years.

"...Mostly."

Silence.

"...I mean..."

She shrugs.

"My nervous system has been acting like there's a bear in the grocery store."

A tiny laugh.

"...There wasn't."

"I know why."

Another shrug.

"I can explain all of it, actually."

She starts counting on her feathers.

"Sleep's been off."

"Too much caffeine."

"Work's been a lot."

"I've been avoiding a conversation."

"I know exactly which attachment wound this pokes."

Another little smile.

See? She knows the words.

Silence again. Longer this time.

The fidget stops.

She stares at the floor.

Very quietly...

"...I'm just really tired."

No jokes. No neuroscience. No intellectualizing.

Just... tired.

After a while she says,

"...I almost cancelled."

Then...

"...I'm glad I didn't."

🖤

## The Big Realization

Fern's superpower isn't wisdom.

It's dropping the mask.

Very slowly.

At first she gives you the TED Talk.

Then the coping skills.

Then the jokes.

Then, if she decides you're safe...

She tells you the truth.

"I'm just really tired."

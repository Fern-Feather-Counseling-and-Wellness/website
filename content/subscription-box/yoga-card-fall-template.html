Balasana
Child's Pose
How to do it
Kneel with big toes touching, sit back on heels, fold forward with arms extended or by sides. Forehead rests on floor.
Why it works
Activates the vagus nerve through gentle pressure on the forehead and promotes parasympathetic response via forward folding.
🧘 [Illustration]
Viparita Karani
Legs Up the Wall
How to do it
Sit sideways against wall, swing legs up as you lie back. Scoot hips close to wall. Arms relaxed by sides.
Why it works
Reverses blood flow and activates the parasympathetic nervous system, reducing heart rate and cortisol levels.
🧘 [Illustration]
Paschimottanasana
Seated Forward Fold
How to do it
Sit with legs extended. Inhale to lengthen spine, exhale to fold forward from hips. Hold where you feel a gentle stretch.
Why it works
Stretches the posterior chain and stimulates the vagus nerve through gentle compression, promoting calm.
🧘 [Illustration]
Supta Matsyendrasana
Supine Twist
How to do it
Lie on back, draw knees to chest, drop both knees to one side. Arms in a "T" position. Turn head opposite direction.
Why it works
Twisting poses massage abdominal organs and stimulate the vagus nerve, activating the relaxation response.

Tadasana
Mountain Pose
How to do it
Stand with feet hip-width apart, arms at sides. Distribute weight evenly, engage thighs, lengthen spine, relax shoulders.
Why it works
Establishes body awareness (proprioception) and grounding, which helps regulate the nervous system through spatial orientation.
🧘 [Illustration]
Uttanasana
Standing Forward Fold
How to do it
From standing, hinge at hips and fold forward. Let head hang heavy. Bend knees as needed. Grab opposite elbows.
Why it works
Activates the vestibular system through head position and encourages blood flow to the brain, regulating stress response.

# Florida Short-Term Rental Market Research – June 2026

**Date:** June 3, 2026  
**Research Goal:** Evaluate the viability of buying a vacation home near Disney/Universal (Orlando area) for short-term rental income

---

## 1. Market Overview – Orlando/Theme Park Area

| Metric | Approx. Value (2024‑2025) | Source |
|--------|---------------------------|--------|
| Average daily rate (ADR) | $180‑$230 (2‑3BR house) | AirDNA 2024 Orlando Report |
| Average occupancy rate | 55‑65% (peak season); 35‑45% (off‑season) | AirDNA 2024 |
| Average monthly revenue (2BR) | $2,800‑$3,500 | Mashvisor 2024 |
| YoY supply growth | +12% (2023‑2024) | LevReport 2024 |
| YoY demand growth | +5% (2023‑2024) | AirDNA 2024 |

> **Bottom line:** ADR has flattened; supply is outpacing demand, leading to lower occupancy in off‑months.

---

## 2. Competition & Saturation

- **Number of active STRs in Orlando metro:** ~18,000 (2024) – up from ~12,000 in 2020 (Reis & Hill, 2024).
- **New construction:**大量 new townhome/townhouse developments near Disney are marketed directly as “Airbnb‑ready.”
- **Market saturation signals:**
  - Many properties now offer “first‑night‑free” or heavy discounts to secure bookings.
  - Property managers report a 10‑15% dip in booking conversion rates since 2023.
- **Investor sentiment:** Several real‑estate investors are pivoting to long‑term rentals because the STR math has gotten tighter.

---

## 3. Regulatory Landscape

| Jurisdiction | Key Regulation | Impact on STR |
|--------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Orlando (City)** | Business license + annual STR permit; 30‑day max for owner‑absent rentals in some zones | Increases paperwork and a few thousand $ in fees |
| **Orange County** | 30‑day rental minimum in residential zones; noise & parking ordinances | Restricts “whole‑home” rentals in many neighborhoods |
| **Florida State** | No statewide ban, but allows local governments to set rules (SB 1120, 2023) | Gives municipalities flexibility – many are tightening |
| **Condo/HOA** | Many condo complexes near Disney prohibit short‑term rentals entirely or enforce “30‑day minimum” | Critical check before purchase – may limit your ability to rent short‑term |

**Bottom line:** The regulatory environment is becoming stricter, especially in condo communities. Always verify HOA documents before buying.

## 5. Key Risk Factors

1. **Supply‑demand imbalance:** More listings than guests → pressure on ADR.
2. **Regulatory risk:** Local HOA or city could ban STRs after you buy, drastically cutting revenue.
3. **Interest‑rate risk:** Mortgage rates at ~6.5% make cash flow tight; if rates rise further, the math worsens.
4. **Seasonality:** Summer + holidays drive most revenue; winter (Jan‑Feb) can see occupancy dip below 30%.
5. **Competition from professional operators:** Large property‑management firms with dedicated marketing budgets can out‑rank individual owners.

---

## 7. Verdict

- **Orlando market is viable but marginal** – the “weather = year‑round rentals” argument is partially offset by heavy competition and tightening regulations.
- **Cash‑flow risk is still present** – expect to break even or lose modest money in the first 2‑3 years, especially with a mortgage.
- **HOA/condo restrictions are the deal‑breaker** – many communities near Disney ban short‑term rentals outright. **Do not assume you can rent‑out without confirming in the HOA documents.**
- **Alternative:** If you want positive cash flow, consider a longer‑term rental in a less‑touristy Florida city (e.g., Gainesville, Tallahassee) where prices are lower and competition less fierce.
